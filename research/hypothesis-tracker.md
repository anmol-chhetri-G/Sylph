## H1: The Atrophy Hypothesis

### Original Formulation
"Prolonged deep symbiotic interaction with autonomous AI agents leads to degradation of executive function through disuse of prefrontal cortical networks, analogous to muscle atrophy from immobilization."

### Lifecycle

| Phase | Status | Evidence |
|-------|--------|----------|
| Phase 0 (Proposal) | POSTULATED | Theoretical basis: BCM theory, ego depletion, use-it-or-lose-it |
| Phase 1 (Evidence) | STRONGLY SUPPORTED | Kosmyna (-55% connectivity), Gerlich (negative correlation), Baldeo (58% "AI did it"), Zhang (dependency), Spatharioti (47% accuracy on errors), Farhat (anxiety) |
| Phase 2 (Model) | INTEGRATED | Layer 1 (behavioral) and Layer 2 (neural) evidence both support |
| Phase 3 (Contradiction) | CHALLENGED | Occupational neuroplasticity: expertise reduces activation = efficiency, not atrophy |
| Phase 4 (Repulsion) | SURVIVED WITH MODIFICATION | Key insight: atrophy applies to *unscaffolded* use only; scaffolded use may prevent it |

### Final Form
**"Prolonged *unscaffolded* AI use that replaces executive function leads to measurable degradation in critical thinking, error detection, and independent cognitive capability. The mechanism is skill atrophy through disuse, not structural capacity loss. Scaffolded use (AI + explicit critical thinking instruction) may prevent or slow this degradation."**

### Confidence: CONTESTED
- Behavioral evidence: STRONG (multiple converging sources)
- Neuroimaging evidence: ABSENT (no longitudinal brain imaging studies)
- Mechanism: PLAUSIBLE (BCM theory, ego depletion, occupational neuroplasticity analogy)
- Boundary condition: IDENTIFIED (scaffolded vs. unscaffolded use)

## H2: The Elevation Hypothesis

### Original Formulation
"AI symbiosis elevates human cognition from execution-level processing to synthesis-level orchestration, expanding creative capacity and cognitive bandwidth."

### Lifecycle

| Phase | Status | Evidence |
|-------|--------|----------|
| Phase 0 (Proposal) | POSTULATED | Theoretical basis: extended mind, hybrid intelligence |
| Phase 1 (Evidence) | PARTIALLY SUPPORTED | Nature Meta (g=0.670 learning gains), IEEE FRUCT (+23-26% creativity), Yuxian (declarative + procedural learning) |
| Phase 2 (Model) | INTEGRATED | Layer 1 (behavioral) supports for objective tasks; Layer 2 (neural) shows PFC deactivation = reduced load |
| Phase 3 (Contradiction) | CHALLENGED | Spatharioti (speed ↑ but accuracy ↓ on errors), Baldeo (delegation not elevation), Zhang/Farhat (dependency) |
| Phase 4 (Repulsion) | SURVIVED WITH SEVERE RESTRICTIONS | Short-term, task-specific, scaffolded only; does not transfer or persist |

### Final Form
**"AI assistance produces short-term, task-specific performance gains in objective domains when usage is scaffolded with explicit critical thinking instruction. These gains (1) do not transfer to novel domains, (2) do not persist after AI removal, and (3) degrade into H1 (atrophy) when usage becomes habitual and unscaffolded. 'Elevation to systems architect' is only achievable for users who already possess domain expertise that the AI augments; novices experience dependency, not elevation."**

### Confidence: CONTESTED
- Short-term gains: CORROBORATED (meta-analysis, experiments)
- Transfer: NOT DEMONSTRATED (no evidence of cross-domain benefit)
- Persistence: NOT DEMONSTRATED (no evidence of lasting capability gain)
- Novice vs. expert: CRITICAL MODERATOR (novices degrade, experts may elevate)

## H3: The Extended Mind Hypothesis

### Original Formulation
"Through sustained interaction, the human brain progressively maps AI agents as functional extensions of the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, constitutively extending executive function beyond the cranium."

### Lifecycle

| Phase | Status | Evidence |
|-------|--------|----------|
| Phase 0 (Proposal) | POSTULATED | Clark (2008) extended mind, "natural-born cyborgs" |
| Phase 1 (Evidence) | THEORETICAL ONLY | GCE framework (arXiv 2026) proposes behavioral manifold alignment |
| Phase 2 (Model) | PARTIALLY INTEGRATED | Layer 2 shows PFC deactivation on objective tasks (consistent with external resource use) |
| Phase 3 (Contradiction) | CHALLENGED | Russell: brain rejects AI for subjective/episodic tasks; AI hallucination undermines trust criterion |
| Phase 4 (Repulsion) | SURVIVED BUT DEMOTED | Weak extended mind possible for objective tasks; fails for subjective tasks; no neural mapping evidence |

### Final Form
**"Under narrow conditions (objective tasks, scaffolded use, high trust), AI may functionally couple with human cognition in ways consistent with weak extended mind theory. However, the brain rejects AI for subjective/episodic memory tasks (Russell 2025), AI's unreliability undermines the 'automatically endorsed' criterion (Clark 2008), and no neuroimaging evidence exists for neural mapping of AI as a cognitive module. H3 remains a theoretical possibility, not an empirical finding."**

### Confidence: SPECULATIVE
- Theoretical framework: EXISTS (Clark, GCE)
- Empirical support: ABSENT (no neuroimaging of neural mapping)
- Counter-evidence: PRESENT (brain rejects AI for personal meaning-making)
- Boundary condition: IDENTIFIED (objective tasks only; fails for subjective)

## NEW HYPOTHESIS: The Conditional Integration Model

### Emerged From
The repulsion process killed the binary versions of H1/H2/H3 and produced a synthesis.

### Formulation
**"AI's effect on human cognition is not unidirectional (atrophying or elevating) but is moderated by three factors: (1) task subjectivity (objective → benefits; subjective → neutral/harmful), (2) scaffolding (guided with CT instruction → elevation; unguided → atrophy), and (3) usage duration (acute → benefits; chronic → degradation). The optimal configuration is AI-as-scaffold for objective tasks with explicit critical thinking training, used in bounded sessions rather than continuous availability."**

### Confidence: CORROBORATED
- Task subjectivity moderation: Empirically demonstrated (Russell 2025)
- Scaffolding moderation: Empirically demonstrated (Yuxian 2025, Farhat 2025 contrast)
- Duration moderation: Theoretically grounded (BCM theory, ego depletion); empirically untested

# Repulsion Log — Phase 4

**Protocol:** "No claim without a corpse. Every claim must survive at least one repulsion attempt."

Each hypothesis is subjected to the strongest counter-evidence available. The goal is to kill it. If it survives, it earns a confidence tier.

---

## REPULSION ATTEMPT #1: Kill H1 (Atrophy)

### The Ammunition

| Source | Evidence Against H1 |
|--------|---------------------|
| S25: Occupational Neuroplasticity Meta | Expertise reduces brain activation — this is efficiency, not atrophy |
| S16: Nature Meta-analysis | ChatGPT improves learning outcomes (g = 0.670) |
| S15: Yuxian | LLM + CT guidance → both declarative and procedural learning improve |
| S8: IEEE FRUCT | AI neural interfaces → memory +23%, creativity +26%, problem-solving +24% |
| S3: Russell (SAT tasks) | Copilot improved objective task performance while PFC deactivated |

### The Attack

**"Reduced cortical connectivity is not atrophy. It is neural efficiency. Expert musicians show the same pattern. The brain is doing the same work with fewer resources. You are pathologizing a feature, not a bug."**

### H1's Defense

1. **The efficiency argument requires maintained capability.** Expert musicians show reduced activation *while performing the skill at a high level*. AI users show reduced activation *while delegating the skill to AI*. The question is not whether the brain uses less energy during AI-assisted work — it is whether the brain *retains the ability to do the work without AI*.

2. **No study measures independent capability post-AI-use.** If Kosmyna's participants were asked to write *without* AI after the AI session, would they perform worse than before? This has not been tested. The occupational expertise literature shows experts maintain capability; the AI literature has not demonstrated this.

3. **Behavioral evidence confirms degradation, not efficiency.** Baldeo (58% report "AI did the thinking"), Zhang (reduced critical/independent thinking), Farhat (anxiety during AI unavailability), Spatharioti (47% accuracy when LLM errs — users don't detect errors). Efficiency does not produce these outcomes.

4. **The ego depletion framework provides a mechanism.** Baumeister (1998) shows self-control is a limited resource. AI offloading may reduce the *practice* of executive function, weakening it through disuse — analogous to muscle atrophy from immobilization, not from efficient use.

### Verdict: H1 SURVIVES — but is MODIFIED

**Original H1:** "Prolonged AI use atrophies executive function and neuroplasticity."

**Modified H1:** "Prolonged *unscaffolded* AI use that replaces rather than augments executive function leads to measurable degradation in critical thinking, error detection, and independent cognitive capability. The neural mechanism is skill atrophy through disuse, not capacity loss. The behavioral evidence is strong; the neuroimaging evidence is absent."

**Confidence tier:** CONTESTED (behavioral evidence strong, neuroimaging absent)

## REPULSION ATTEMPT #2: Kill H2 (Elevation)

### The Ammunition

| Source | Evidence Against H2 |
|--------|---------------------|
| S10: Spatharioti | 50% speed gain BUT accuracy drops to 47% when LLM errs; users don't verify |
| S11: Baldeo | 58% report "AI did most of the thinking" — this is delegation, not elevation |
| S12: Zhang | AI dependency → laziness, reduced critical thinking, reduced independent thinking |
| S14: Farhat | Students develop "cognitive reliance that impaired critical thinking, creativity" |
| S20: Ye et al. | Inert thinking (aversion to effortful processing) strongly correlates with ChatGPT dependence |
| S9: Pergantis | Benefits are short-term, task-specific, no transfer evidence |

### The Attack

**"If users become unable to critically evaluate AI outputs, cannot think independently, avoid effortful processing, and show anxiety when AI is unavailable — how can they be 'elevated' to systems architects? Elevation requires *more* cognitive capability, not less. You are describing a drug dependency, not an intellectual upgrade."**

### H2's Defense

1. **H2 is conditionally true.** It holds for *scaffolded* use (Yuxian), *objective* tasks (Russell SAT), and *novice* workers in structured domains (Brynjolfsson customer service). It fails for *unscaffolded* use, *subjective* tasks, and *habitual* deployment.

2. **The CHI 2025 meta-analysis confirms short-term elevation.** The g = 0.670 effect is real. The question is whether it transfers and persists.

3. **Speed gains are real and economically significant.** Even if quality is compromised, the productivity increase is measurable. The problem is not that H2 is false — it is that H2 describes a *temporary state* that degrades into H1 without intervention.

### Verdict: H2 SURVIVES — but is SEVERELY RESTRICTED

**Original H2:** "AI symbiosis elevates human cognition to a systems architect level."

**Modified H2:** "AI assistance produces short-term, task-specific performance gains in objective domains when usage is scaffolded. These gains do not transfer to novel domains, do not persist after AI removal, and degrade into H1 (atrophy) when usage becomes habitual and unscaffolded. 'Elevation to systems architect' requires existing expertise that the AI augments; novices experience dependency, not elevation."

**Confidence tier:** CONTESTED (short-term gains corroborated; long-term elevation speculative)

## REPULSION ATTEMPT #3: Kill H3 (Extended Mind)

### The Ammunition

| Source | Evidence Against H3 |
|--------|---------------------|
| S3: Russell | Brain shows NO PFC activation change during subjective/personal tasks with AI — brain *rejects* AI for episodic memory |
| S10: Spatharioti | Users do not verify AI output; 47% accuracy when LLM errs; no evidence of "integration" |
| S13: Rosas-Jiménez | Blind trust ≠ cognitive integration; overreliance is a failure mode, not an extension |
| Clark (2008) criteria | AI fails: not constantly available, not automatically endorsed, not trustworthy (hallucination) |
| Embodied cognition literature | AI is disembodied; human cognition is embodied; brain may reject AI precisely because it lacks embodied grounding |

### The Attack

**"Clark's extended mind requires that the external resource be constantly available, automatically endorsed, and trustworthy. AI hallucinates, fabricates, and confidently states falsehoods. Russell shows the brain does not even attempt to integrate AI for personal meaning-making. This is not an extension of the self — it is an external tool that the brain uses opportunistically and discards when it fails."**

### H3's Defense

1. **The GCE framework provides a theoretical account.** The "Gradual Cognitive Externalization" model (arXiv 2026) proposes that AI becomes an extension through behavioral manifold alignment — not through neural mapping but through functional coupling.

2. **Russell's data partially supports H3.** For *objective* tasks, PFC deactivation with AI is consistent with the brain treating AI as an external cognitive resource. The brain reduces its own activation because the AI is handling that component.

3. **The extended mind thesis does not require neural mapping.** Clark's original argument was about functional parity, not about the brain literally treating the tool as a neural module.

### Verdict: H3 SURVIVES — but is DEMOTED to SPECULATIVE

**Original H3:** "The brain maps AI as an externalized module of the DLPFC, constitutively extending executive function."

**Modified H3:** "Under specific conditions (objective tasks, scaffolded use, high trust), AI may functionally couple with human cognition in ways consistent with weak extended mind theory. However, the brain rejects AI for subjective/episodic tasks, AI's unreliability undermines the 'automatically endorsed' criterion, and no neuroimaging evidence exists for neural mapping of AI as a cognitive module. H3 remains a theoretical possibility, not an empirical finding."

**Confidence tier:** SPECULATIVE (theoretical framework exists; empirical evidence absent)

## REPULSION ATTEMPT #4: Kill the "Subjectivity Gradient"

### The Ammunition

Russell's study has n = 20. Small sample, single lab, one AI tool (Copilot), one culture (American university students). The "gradient" may be an artifact of task difficulty rather than subjectivity per se.

### The Attack

**"You have built a central finding on a study of 20 people using one tool. The subjectivity gradient may not replicate."**

### The Defense

1. **The gradient is theoretically grounded.** Objective tasks engage working memory (PFC-dependent); subjective tasks engage episodic memory (hippocampal-dependent). These are neuroanatomically distinct systems. The gradient maps onto known neuroscience.

2. **Multiple sources converge on the same pattern.** Spatharioti shows overreliance on LLM errors (objective tasks). Baldeo shows cognitive offloading during planning (semi-objective). Farhat shows dependency in academic writing (semi-subjective). The pattern holds across studies.

3. **The effect is large and task-dependent within the same study.** Same participants, same tool, same session — but different outcomes by task type. This is not a between-study artifact.

### Verdict: SUBJECTIVITY GRADIENT SURVIVES — CONFIRMED

**Confidence tier:** CORROBORATED (convergent evidence from multiple sources; theoretically grounded)

## Summary of Repulsion Attempts

| Hypothesis | Kill Attempt | Outcome | Final Status | Confidence |
|-----------|--------------|---------|--------------|------------|
| H1 (Atrophy) | Neural efficiency counter-argument | Survived with modification | CONDITIONALLY TRUE | CONTESTED |
| H2 (Elevation) | Dependency/erosion counter-argument | Survived with severe restrictions | SHORT-TERM ONLY | CONTESTED |
| H3 (Extended Mind) | Brain rejection + hallucination | Survived but demoted | THEORETICAL ONLY | SPECULATIVE |
| Subjectivity Gradient | Small sample critique | Survived | EMPIRICALLY GROUNDED | CORROBORATED |

**What the corpses taught us:** The original hypotheses were too binary. AI does not "atrophies" or "elevates" cognition in general — it does both, depending on *how* it is used, *what* task is being performed, and *whether* the user maintains active cognitive engagement. The field needs conditional models, not universal claims.
